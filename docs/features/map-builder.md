# Map builder

Status: built.

The builder makes a board: a mat, its rotation and a list of terrain pieces, in the map JSON format (`maps.md`, "JSON format"). It lives in the Sandbox (Build mode). The dev page `#terrain` is gone.

## Shared code

- `src/terrain/builder.js`: the edit actions as pure functions on a plain list of placements (add, move, turn, remove, clear, load a map, export). They return a new list. Reason: the Sandbox keeps the list in the table document, so the actions must not own the state.
- `src/terrain/BuilderPanel.jsx`: the side panel. It takes the board data and callbacks as props and owns only the piece filter.
- `src/terrain/useTerrainDrag.js`: the drag of a placed piece. `src/terrain/useBuilderKeys.js`: Q, E and Delete.

## Add

A new piece stands with the bottom of its mesh on the mat top. The piece is a model whose origin is often in the middle of the mesh, so a position at the mat top would put half of it under the mat. The height comes from the bounding box of the mesh with the placement scale and turn, as the piece is drawn (`restY` in `src/terrain/placementMove.js`). The piece files load async, so the add waits for the box (`src/terrain/pieceBox.js`) and then places the piece at its final height. It never shows at a wrong height first. The piece goes to the mat center, turned 0°.

## Drag

The drag plane is the horizontal plane through the point where the press grabbed the piece. The grabbed point stays under the pointer, also when the piece is grabbed on its side or top and the camera looks at an angle. A plane at the mat top would not do this: the move on that plane is not the move under the pointer. The height of the piece does not change, and the piece does not climb on other pieces. A drag moves the piece on the table only; the drop writes the position and mounts the piece again with its fixed body. The move is taken in the frame of the mat group, so it follows the pointer when the mat is turned. Only one terrain drag runs at a time. Esc puts the piece back.

## Board in the document

The table document holds the board (`rooms.md`, "Storage"; `peer-to-peer.md`, "Yjs document").

- A stored terrain piece is a map piece `{ id, index, locked, y? }` or a custom piece `{ id, piece, position, rotation, scale, size?, tint?, locked, y?, index? }`. A piece with `piece` is custom, whatever else it has. `withPlacements` in `src/rooms/table.js` reads both and keeps the same object for an unchanged piece. Reason: old tables keep working, a fix of the map data still reaches map pieces, and a board with no map can be stored.
- A map piece that the builder moves or turns becomes a custom piece: `toCustomPiece(mapId, stored)` gives it with the same id, lock and placement, and it keeps its `index` (the export reads the placement note there). Map pieces need a map: with `mapId` null they are left out, custom pieces stay. A custom piece whose `piece` key `TERRAIN_PIECES` does not have (a peer on another build, bad data) is left out too, so the scene never looks up a missing piece.
- `game.mat` is `{ name, rotation }` or null. `tableMat(mapId, mat)` gives the mat of a table (null: the mat and rotation of the map; no map and no mat: none). Every reader uses it: the scene, and the preload (`tableFiles`).

## Sandbox: Empty mat

The Map select of the Sandbox has two groups: **Maps** and **Empty mat** (one row per mat, by name). An empty mat sets `game.mat` and clears the terrain in one change, and `mapId` becomes null. A map sets `game.mat` back to null, so the mat of the map shows. A board with no map but with pieces (built or imported) shows as a disabled row, **Custom board**. Reason: the select must name what is on the table.

## Sandbox: Build mode

The **Build** button next to the Map select (Sandbox only) turns Build mode on and off. A side panel (`BuilderPanel.jsx`) opens on the right.

- **Unlock is a view flag.** In Build mode every terrain piece acts unlocked: it can be hovered, selected and dragged. The locks in the document are not written, so leaving Build mode locks the pieces again with no write, and a piece the player unlocked with L stays unlocked. A piece added in Build mode is stored locked, for the same reason.
- **Click** selects a piece, a **drag** moves it (see "Drag"), **Q / E** turn the selected piece by 15°, **Delete** removes it. Delete is the same removal as the game's (`handleTerrainRemove`): what stood on the piece drops down, the log has an Undo. Models, tools and dice stay as they are.
- **Keys.** In Build mode the game's Q / E (turn a character or tool) and Delete (remove the hovered thing) do nothing (`keyAction`, state `building`), and the builder's keys act on the selected terrain piece. Outside Build mode nothing changes: Delete removes an unlocked hovered terrain piece as before, and Q / E do not turn terrain. The builder keys are fixed to Q, E and Delete / Backspace (they do not follow a rebinding), and do nothing while a popup is open or a field has the focus.
- A moved or turned map piece becomes a custom piece (`toCustomPiece`). Every edit writes the stored pieces and `game.mat`. A move or turn raises `rev` of the piece (stored), so the other browser mounts the piece again with its fixed body.
- **Add** writes a custom piece at the mat center, at its rest height (see "Add").
- **Panel:** the mat list (sets `game.mat`, keeps the pieces), **Clear** (removes all pieces at once; models that stood on them do not drop), the filter, the pieces by size with the scanned ones, **Load map** (sets the map and its terrain, `game.mat` null), the `name` field (required), **Export** (the map JSON file, with the mat of the table), **Import**, and, only when logged in, **Save to account** and **Load from account**.
- **Mat list:** one row per mat, by name. A click shows the mat, turned by the `matRotation` of the first map that uses it (`src/terrain/catalog.js`). A new mat keeps the placed pieces, and the pieces do not turn with the mat (the placements are relative to the mat center). A fresh board (no map loaded) has an empty `name`.
- **Piece list:** a filter field (by piece name) and the pieces in groups: size 1 to 5, then "No size". A group header shows the number of pieces. A piece with two sizes is in both groups. A piece with a default `size` that no map places (a scan piece) is in the group of that size, with scale 1 and no tint. A click on a row adds the piece with the scale and tint that its placements at that size use most often (`catalog.js`), and selects it. A click on the empty table clears the selection.
- **Load map** keeps `id`, `name`, `ttsMap`, `note` and `skipped` for the export. A note on a placement stays on it, also after the piece was moved: the document stores only the `index`, and the export reads the note from the map data (`mapExportState` in `builder.js`, used by Export and Save to account). A table with no map exports none of these fields. The builder does not edit these fields.
- **Name and id.** The `name` is required (not empty after trim): **Export** and **Save to account** are disabled while it is empty, with a title that says why. There is no `id` field. The id is made from the name (`boardIdFromName` in `builder.js`): lower case, runs of other characters become one hyphen, no hyphen at the ends, cut to a short length, then `-` and a random 4-character suffix (e.g. `my-cool-map-k3x9`). The name, the author, the fixed id and the suffix are the board record in the table document (`game.board`, `newBoard` in `rooms/table.js`), not the panel state, so a re-export or re-save keeps the id even after a Build toggle or a reload. The suffix is made once per record, and a rename changes only the slug part. Every map choice (Map select, Load map) and every import writes a new record in the same change as the board. A table from before the record has none, which reads as an empty name; the first name creates it. Reason: the id is the file name and must be kebab-case and not clash with another board. A map loaded from **Load map** keeps its own id, and an imported file or a board loaded from the account keeps the id of the data (an empty id: made from the name).
- **Author.** The map JSON has an optional `author` (`maps.md`). **Export** writes the name of the logged-in user, else the `author` of an imported file, else none. **Save to account** sets `author` on the server from the user record (never from the body), and `GET /me/board` returns it. Import keeps it for the next export.
- **Logged out.** **Save to account** and **Load from account** show only when the session status is `in` (`useUser`; not while `loading`, `out`, `error` or `off`). The session is a global store, so the Sandbox needs no provider.
- `src/terrain/exportMap.js` writes the file: the field order and number rounding of the migrate script (positions 3 decimals, rotations 2), 2-space indent, trailing newline. The script uses the same writer. The builder-only fields (`id`, `rev`, `locked`) are dropped. A loaded map that is exported without change gives its file byte for byte (a test checks all maps).
- Each piece has its own Suspense, so a piece that loads its files does not hide the others.

## Import

**Import** reads a `.json` file in the map format. `validateBoard` (`src/terrain/builder.js`) checks it before anything changes: an object, a known mat name, a list of at most 300 placements, each with a piece key of `TERRAIN_PIECES`, a position and rotation of 3 numbers, a scale, and optional size and tint. A good file sets `game.mat` from the file (the rotation of the mat when the file has none), makes its placements custom pieces with new ids, locked, sets `mapId` to null and fills the `name` field and keeps the file `id` and `author`. The `id` is kept only when it is kebab-case and not longer than the api allows (`MAP_ID_PATTERN`, `MAP_ID_MAX`, `src/terrain/exportMap.js`), because it is the file name and Save to account must take it; an id made from the name always fits; any other id is dropped and the id is made from the name. A bad file shows its first problem in the panel and changes nothing. The `ttsMap`, `note` and `skipped` fields of a file are not kept.

## Save in the account

A logged-in player keeps one board in the account (`docs/features/auth.md`, "Endpoints"). **Save to account** sends the export object of the current board (`exportBoard`, the same as the Export file) with `PUT /me/board`. **Load from account** reads `GET /me/board` and runs the same path as Import (`useBuilderEdits`, `importData`: `validateBoard`, then `applyBoard`). The panel shows a line when there is no saved board or a call fails. Saving again replaces the board, and its top view (next section). The API checks the shape again (`apps/api/src/routes/board.ts`, with a copy of the id pattern and max) and stores the fields one by one. A board saved before the id check may have a bad id: `GET /me/board` still returns it, and the client check drops it, so the next save sends an id made from the name. `DELETE /me` deletes the board.

## Top view image

Save to account also sends a top view of the board, so the "My board" card can show it like the map cards do. It is a 512 x 512 px WebP of the mat square, made in the browser by `captureBoardImage` (`src/terrain/boardImage.js`), called through `sceneApi.captureBoardImage` because it needs the renderer and the scene of the Canvas. Nothing changes on screen.

- The mat and the terrain are drawn once from straight above, by an orthographic camera that frames exactly the mat square, into an off-screen render target. Every other object (models, trays, tokens, tools) is hidden for that one draw and shown again in `finally`; the lights stay, so the board has the light it has on the table. The draw and the restore are one synchronous block, so no frame shows the hidden state.
- No models, no shadows of models, no selection shade (the orange tint of a selected piece with Faster drawing on). Reason: the image shows the board, not the game on it. Shadows are drawn on demand (`docs/features/rendering.md`), so the capture asks for a new shadow map after it hides the others. The shade is off for the one draw (`withoutShades`, `src/scene/shade.js`); `useOutline` stays its owner.
- The image shows the board as it is saved (`matTurns` 0): the camera turns with the mat group, so a turned mat gives the same image. Up in the image is the -z side of the table.
- The look matches the table: the linear image gets the tone mapping of the look (`look.toneMapping`) and the sRGB conversion in one extra pass, because three.js does neither for a render target.
- A browser that cannot encode WebP, or a save with no mat drawn, saves the board without an image. The panel says so.
- The account keeps the image in the board record (`docs/features/backend.md`, "Collections"). A save without an image removes the old one: the image always belongs to the saved board.

## Board in a new room

A logged-in player with a saved board (`GET /me/board`, checked with `validateBoard`) sees a card "My board" after the maps in the new room dialog. It is drawn by `BoardCard` (`src/rooms/BoardCard.jsx`): the blank map card front as background (`src/assets/maps/front.webp`, rounded corners in CSS; no card back). The front is the blank card of Jarvis (`https://www.jarvis-protocol.com/assets/images/f61a599248bd78da3ca3.png`, red) turned to the green of the map cards: `magick card-red.png -modulate 100,125,138.3 -resize x1400 -gravity center -crop 800x1400+0+0 +repage -channel RGB -level 0%,100%,0.85 +channel`, then WebP quality 85. The content is, in the places of the map cards: the board name as the title (bold condensed capitals, no number badge) ("My board" when the board has none), under it the fixed line "Design Intent: Competitive" and, one line lower, "Creator: <author>" (the author the server set on the board; a saved board without an author has no Creator line), then the fixed line "Source: Hero Table 3D Sandbox Map Builder" (one more line lower, or in the Creator line's place without an author; it wraps to a second line if too long, no ellipsis), and the top view in the square at the bottom, with a green rounded border. A board saved without an image shows the same front with an empty green square. The dialog fetches the image with the token (`getBoardImage`, an object URL that is revoked on unmount), because an `img` tag cannot send it. No login, no saved board, or a saved board that fails the check: no card. Reason: the board needs the account to be fetched, and a bad record must not open a broken table.

- A room on it has `mapId: 'custom'`. `startTable(mapId, board)` (`src/rooms/table.js`) gives `game.mat` of the board and its placements as custom pieces (`boardPieces`, ids `custom:<n>`).
- The board is copied into the room at creation. Solo: the checked board is in the room record (`board`, with its name and author), and the table is built from it when the room opens. Versus: the host builds the document with the board, and the guest gets it with the document sync. The room record of a versus room has no copy of the board, so a table that would need the start table again (an empty table, or an old one that cannot be migrated) cannot be rebuilt: the session leaves for the lobby with "The board of this room can not be rebuilt." (`src/rooms/openTableSession.js`) instead of opening a table with no mat and no terrain. A later save of the board changes no existing room.
- The room also keeps its own copy of the name, the author and the top view, so the lobby tile shows the card (`RoomBoardCard`, the same `BoardCard`). Solo: the name and author are in the record, the image is copied from the account (`getBoardImage`) into IndexedDB keyed by the room code (`src/rooms/roomImage.js`). Delete removes it, and the lobby cleanup removes images of rooms that are gone. Versus: the server copies the name, author and image from the board of the host into the room record when `POST /rooms` has `mapId: 'custom'`. The client sends nothing extra, so there is no upload and no client value to trust. Reason: the board of the account is already on the server, and the host is the same user. The client fetches the image with `GET /rooms/{code}/image` as a blob (object URL, revoked on unmount). The same hook serves the dialog, the tile and the join page (`useBoardImageUrl`). A room made when the account has no image (or no board on the server) shows the same front with an empty green square.
- A random room never gets the board: the `maps` list and the random pick use the maps of `MAPS` only (`MAP_IDS` in `src/rooms/myBoard.js`). `fillMap` does nothing for `'custom'`, because the table has a `mapId` already.
- `'custom'` is not a key of `MAPS`. A reader of `mapId` checks `isPlayableMap` (room list, room page) or handles the missing key (`withPlacements`, `tableMat`, `fillMap`). The lobby tile, the join page and the delete confirm show the card of the room copy (`BoardCard`) and its name (`mapFace(mapId, boardName)`); "My board" only when the copy has no name (a room made before the copies). The new room dialog shows the author on the card only, not again in the nav box.
